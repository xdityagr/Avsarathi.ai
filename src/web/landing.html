The cheapest credit in India
reaches the fewest people.

NSFDC lends to Scheduled Caste borrowers at 6.5% a year — below any commercial rate — through 102 channel partners. Most people who qualify never hear of it, and those who do can't tell which of five schemes fits, or which partner is able to disburse.

What it does

Checks eligibility, and explains the misses

Income and project cost are matched against every published scheme. You're told which ones you don't qualify for and exactly which threshold you missed — that's the part people actually learn from.

Prices the loan honestly

NSFDC repays quarterly, not monthly, so you see the instalment you'll really be asked for and a monthly figure to budget against — plus the total the loan costs over its life.

Routes to a partner who can pay

Applications get misrouted to agencies that can't disburse. Routing runs on NSFDC's own published fund data, and says plainly why an agency was ruled out.

Built on published data, not assumptions

Every scheme number carries the page it came from and the date it was read. Where a figure isn't published anywhere, the tool says so rather than inventing one.

Three things most people miss

The problem statement quotes 6.5% to 15% “depending on the scheme”. Three products cannot span that. Five can, exactly — and two of them are missing from most descriptions of this programme. Fund-utilisation figures are widely assumed to be internal to NSFDC. They are published as a spreadsheet, state by state, and updated through the year. Routing here runs on that file. Udyam Nidhi costs 13% through a cooperative bank and 15% through a small finance bank. Same scheme, same borrower. Nobody tells applicants this.
